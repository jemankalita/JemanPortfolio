One Tweet Changed Everything

Jeman Kalita June 2026 4 min read

A few weeks ago, I made a post on X that I thought would get a handful of views and maybe a few likes.

Instead, it changed the trajectory of my summer.

The post was simple:

Recently cold-emailed MatiksHQ for an internship as a high schooler.

Built a bot that bypassed part of their backend security and could be used to cheat on the platform. The CTO agreed to a call.

Called twice. Texted. Followed up.

Nothing. Their loss.

I was willing to work unpaid just to learn, build, and contribute.

Their inbox is full. Mine is open.

If you're building in AI, fintech, quant, or software and need someone who's willing to break things, fix things, and learn fast, DM me.

I hit publish and went on with my day.

Then the notifications started rolling in.

The tweet took off.

Founders started reaching out.

Builders started sharing it.

People resonated with the story not because it was some incredible success story, but because it was real.

I wasn't celebrating an internship offer.

I was talking about rejection.

And apparently, a lot of people could relate.

By the end of it, 12 founders had reached out to me directly.

Some were building exciting early-stage startups. Others came from companies I'd only heard about online before. One of the messages was from people at Whop.

The craziest part?

I'm 18 years old.

I haven't even started college yet.

A few weeks before this, I was just another kid sending cold emails and hoping someone would reply.

Now I'm currently doing a work trial at an early-stage startup, getting the opportunity to learn from people who are actually building things.

Not because I had a perfect resume.

Not because I had a degree.

Not because I knew the right people.

Because I posted.

That's the lesson.

People massively underestimate the power of sharing what they're doing online.

Most opportunities don't come from applications anymore.

They come from visibility.

Someone sees your project.

Someone sees your persistence.

Someone sees your curiosity.

And suddenly a door opens that didn't exist yesterday.

The tweet wasn't polished.

It wasn't optimized.

It wasn't some growth-hacking masterpiece.

It was just honest.

I got rejected.

I kept going.

I told the story.

That's it.

Looking back, the biggest takeaway isn't that the tweet went viral.

It's that the internet rewards people who consistently put themselves out there.

You don't know who's watching.

You don't know which founder is scrolling.

You don't know which opportunity is one post away.

If you're building something, learning something, failing at something, or obsessing over something, share it.

Post the project.

Post the lessons.

Post the failures.

Post before you feel ready.

Because sometimes the thing that changes your life isn't the work itself.

It's letting people know that you're doing the work.

For me, this was a reminder that grit compounds.

Every cold email.

Every side project.

Every late-night coding session.

Every rejection.

None of it was wasted.

And while I still have a long way to go, seeing founders reach out, getting opportunities to work with startups, and being trusted to contribute at 18 years old has been a huge personal milestone.

So if you've been hesitating to post your work online, consider this your sign.

Hit publish.

You never know what luck has waiting on the other side.
